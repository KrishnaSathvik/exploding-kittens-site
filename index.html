Exploding Kittens Rules

How to play in 5 minutes: don't draw the bomb, or Defuse it. Last player still in wins.

Learn the rules

Exploding Kittens is a 2–5 player card game you can learn in about 5 minutes. Don't draw an Exploding Kitten — or play a Defuse to survive. The last player who hasn't exploded wins.

This unofficial guide covers every rule in plain language: setup for 2–5 players, all 56 cards and how many of each are in the box, what happens on a turn, the special combos most people miss, and a printable one-page cheat sheet. Defuse

Saves your life! Play this when you draw an Exploding Kitten, then secretly place the kitten back anywhere in the deck.

Nope

5 in deck

Cancel any action card played by anyone. Can be played anytime, even on someone else's turn! Can't stop Exploding Kittens or Defuses.

Tip: You can Nope a Nope! That makes a "Yup" and the original action happens. The chain can go on!

Attack

4 in deck

End your turn without drawing. The next player must now take 2 turns in a row (drawing each time).

Tip: If someone Attacks you, Attack back! Your turns end and the next player takes all remaining turns + 2 more.

Skip

4 in deck

End your turn without drawing a card. If you owe multiple turns (from Attack), this only ends one of them.

Tip: Perfect when you suspect the top card is an Exploding Kitten, especially right after someone defused.

5 in deck

Privately peek at the top 3 cards of the deck. Don't show anyone! Put them back in the same order.

Tip: Peek first — if a Kitten is near the top, play Shuffle or Attack to dodge it. Incredibly powerful combo!

Gameplay

How a Turn Works

Each turn has only 2 phases — super simple:

Phase 1 — Play Cards (Optional)

Play as many action cards from your hand as you want. Or play nothing at all. Each card goes face-up on the discard pile and its effect happens immediately.

Phase 2 — Draw a Card (Mandatory!)

Draw the top card from the deck. If it's safe, add it to your hand — your turn is over. Play passes clockwise.

Drew an Exploding Kitten?

Have a Defuse? Play it! Secretly put the Kitten back anywhere in the deck. You survive!

No Defuse? You explode. Discard all your cards. You're out of the game.

Advanced

Special Combos

Combos work with any matching cards — cats, Skips, Shuffles, anything with the same name. When you play a combo, ignore the cards' normal actions.

2 of a Kind — Any 2 matching cards. Steal a random card from any player's hand.
3 of a Kind — Any 3 matching cards. Name a card; if the target has it, they must give it to you.
5 Different Cards — Take any card you want from the discard pile. Rare but powerful! (Nope can cancel combos too.)
Good to Know

Quick Rules FAQ

Q

Is there a maximum hand size in Exploding Kittens?

No. There is no maximum or minimum. If you run out of cards, keep playing — you'll draw one next turn.

Q

Can the draw pile run out?

Never. There are enough Exploding Kittens to eliminate everyone but the winner first.

Q

Can you look at the discard pile?

Yes. The discard pile is public — anyone can look through it at any time.

Q

What happens if you Attack a player who was already attacked?

Attacks stack. If you're attacked and play Attack, the next player takes 4 turns.

Q

Does a Skip card cancel an Attack?

Only partly. If you owe 2 turns, one Skip ends only one of them. Two Skips end both.

Q

What happens when you draw an Exploding Kitten without a Defuse?

Show it immediately, discard your whole hand, and you are out of the game.

Strategy

Pro Tips to Win

1
Track Defuse cards. Once they're all gone, every single draw becomes life-or-death for everyone.
2
Count the deck. You're allowed to! As it shrinks, Exploding Kittens become more and more likely.
3
Peek then act. Use See the Future before deciding to Shuffle, Skip, or Attack. Knowledge is power.
4
Save your Nopes. Don't waste them early. A Nope on someone's last Defuse is absolutely devastating.
5
Top-deck the Kitten. When you defuse, place it right on top of the deck to blow up the next player.
6
Bluff everything. Act confident even when panicking. A good poker face wins more games than good cards.
